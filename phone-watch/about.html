How this is made

What this is

Phone Watch counts how many people on city streets are using their phones. It looks through public street webcams in 13 cities, once an hour, every day. The numbers on the main page grow with every check.

How it is made

Each camera is a fixed webcam that its owner streams on YouTube for anyone to watch. The list is at the bottom of this page.

Once an hour, a small program on one Mac mini at home takes a single frame from each stream. A vision model called Qwen3-VL 30B-A3B runs on that same Mac. It draws a box around each person close enough to judge, up to 30 people per frame.

Then the program takes a second look. It cuts out each person and makes the picture bigger. The model says if it can see a phone, where the hands are, and if the person is looking at them.

A phone in a hand counts as yes. Both hands together in front, with eyes on them, also counts as yes. People the model cannot judge are marked unclear and are not part of the share.

Every night, a second program turns the counts into this website. It runs on the same Mac and uses no AI service.

What it costs

Each check costs nothing. The model runs on the Mac, not in the cloud, so no photo goes to an AI company and no one pays for each question. A full round of all the cameras takes about half an hour of the computer's time.

What stays private

The model does not know who anyone is. It only sees a box and a phone. The photos stay on the Mac for three days so the counts can be checked, and then they are deleted. The site shows only the counts and the newest frame from each camera, and that frame is replaced every day.

Where it goes wrong

Night, rain, umbrellas, coats and people facing away all hide phones. Some cameras sit high above the street, so the people look small. Read each number as a rough count from one street corner, not a fact about a whole city. We removed the Rio de Janeiro camera because it was too far away to judge anyone.

Thank you

The idea came from the WaldoWorld website. Thank you also to the people who keep these cameras running.
